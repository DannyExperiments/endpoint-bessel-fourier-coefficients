Acosta Babb, *Lᵖ convergence of eigenfunction expansions for the Laplacian on planar domains*, University of Warwick thesis (2024), §5.5, Conjecture 3, printed p. 88. It asks for the weighted integrable function with order-zero normalized coefficients majorizing any nonnegative null sequence and themselves tending to zero. The journal version, *On the rate of growth of Bessel–Fourier coefficients for integrable functions*, JMAA 559(1), 130440, retains this statement conditionally as Corollary 5.2, PDF and printed p. 13. The 2024 arXiv manuscript title is different, and has only v1. Myeongjin Kang's *A two-term counterexample to Bessel–Fourier kernel positivity*, mathdb:2610.00793v1 (paper dated October 27, 2026), gives a distinct negative point at exponent 2/5 and radius 47/50, with a claimed Lean certificate. Its p. 3 explicitly excludes the separate slow-decay existence questions. Both concern the positivity premise, making a different endpoint proof necessary. The present proof uses a radial autocorrelation in place of that particular kernel. The checked scope is based on the two reports; their formal code is outside this replay. - **Classical comparison.** The source traces forward integrability-versus-decay results to Young's *On Series of Bessel Functions*. The classical slow-decay converse for ordinary Fourier coefficients uses positive Fejér kernels for torus characters, as summarized in the journal's Theorem 1.1 and illustrated in Grafakos §3.3. That analogy alone gives neither the weighted Bessel coefficient lower bounds nor convergence after division by the square root of the Bessel zero. The autocorrelation identity is classical. The construction selects dyadic masses adapted to the Bessel normalization and controls both frequency tails. [Young (1920)](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s2-18.1.163) · [Journal introduction](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/199656/1/1-s2.0-S0022247X26000521-main.pdf#page=1)
- **Parity companion.** Journal Conjecture 2 (p. 13), corresponding to dissertation Conjecture 4, asks for one parity of coefficients to vanish and positive lower bounds on the other parity. The journal's conditional Proposition 5.3 already observes the same-sign integral of the partial sums if that pattern holds, using Eq. (2.8). The present companion couples that test with the standard positive, contractive Dirichlet heat semigroup to contradict integrability. It addresses the prescribed one-parity lower bounds, leaving the more general question of unregularized series divergence outside scope.
